# Flickday social voice

Working direction, October 29, 2026. This is a starting point for gallery
announcements, not a measured description of an established voice. Nino asked
for less robotic captions and photos with more story and character. His future
edits should refine this direction; these examples are not reusable templates.

## Sound like someone who notices people

Flickday covers grassroots sports for players, families and the people who put
events together. The photos give them a way to recognize themselves and their
community. The caption should help them see something in the frame, then find
their photos.

The starting voice is observant, warm and lightly funny. Let the humor come
from what people are doing: a crown on a volleyball, an improvised pose, a laugh
that interrupts the setup. Keep the people in the photograph at the center.
The photographer's expertise does not need to be the subject of every post.

This develops the audience and courtside personality in
[BRAND-PRIORS.md](../BRAND-PRIORS.md). It carries factual discipline and reader
awareness from Nino's Signal Dispatch voice guide, without importing an essay's
argument, rhythm or reflective ending into a sports caption.

## Write from the particular photograph

- Open with a detail that belongs to this set. A caption that fits every album
  probably needs another look at the photographs.
- Let warmth or a dry observation do the work. Do not add a joke, slang or hype
  just to sound casual. Never make an athlete the target of the joke.
- Give enough event context to orient someone arriving from their feed. Dates
  and photo counts belong when they help, not as a compulsory opening block.
- End with the useful action. Instagram can point to a verified bio link;
  Facebook gets the direct gallery URL. Photo credit stays short.
- Keep the length and shape flexible. A quiet portrait may need only a line;
  a sequence may earn a little more. There is no required punchline, slogan,
  hashtag block or sentence pattern.

Names, quotes, results and behind-the-scenes stories need a source. A visible
expression can support an observation; it does not reveal what someone thought
or said. Photo alt text stays literal and useful, separate from the social voice.

## First application: 630 Picture Day

The earlier announcement led with the date and "10 of 36 from the gallery,"
then added a slogan, duplicate branding and generic hashtags. Those lines
explained the inventory but gave little reason to look at these particular
photographs.

The revised Instagram caption is:

> Even the volleyball got a crown.
>
> A few from picture day at 630. Some poses required lifting a teammate.
>
> Find your photos in the full gallery. Link in bio.
>
> Photos by Nino Chavez.

Facebook uses the same copy with the direct gallery URL in place of "Link in
bio." The crown and lifted teammate are visible in the selected photos.
The revised selection also replaces two waiting/group frames with a player
pointing at a printed photograph and a laughing candid. The aim is a sequence
with interactions and expressions, not ten interchangeable portraits.

This caption is an applied first example, not evidence that the voice has been
validated. As Nino edits subsequent posts, keep the specific correction and its
reason here. Retire an example when it starts turning into a formula; do not
turn every successful line into a rule.
