# Flickday hybrid raster review — round 3

Status: visual-direction review only. These are not production wordmarks.

All six proofs use the same image-generated core. Each variant was prompted to preserve the exact word `flickday` and change only one graphic cue. Image generation still introduces minor letterform, spacing, and edge drift, so review the custom glyph idea rather than the typography itself.

## Variants

1. `01-core-control.png` — unmodified wordmark control and small-size fallback.
2. `02-aperture-i-photo.png` — a curved six-blade aperture replaces the dot of the `i`.
3. `03-play-d-video.png` — a restrained play symbol is integrated into the `d`.
4. `04-reel-d-film.png` — a five-hole film reel is integrated into the `d`.
5. `05-filmstrip-y-motion.png` — sprocket holes appear only in the descender of the `y`.
6. `06-reel-to-y-expressive.png` — the reel in the `d` releases a filmstrip into the `y`.

## Candid design review

- **Play `d`:** strongest primary video identity. It communicates quickly and survives reduction better than the more detailed options.
- **Aperture `i`:** strongest photography-specific alternate. The curved blades read as an optical iris rather than a generic geometric flower.
- **Core:** necessary control and small watermark. It should remain available wherever a graphic glyph becomes illegible.
- **Filmstrip `y`:** credible motion or editing alternate. Distinctive without substantially destabilizing the word.
- **Reel `d`:** clear, but more literal and conventional. Useful for film-focused contexts rather than as the universal mark.
- **Reel-to-`y`:** most expressive and most complicated. Better for apparel, campaign art, title cards, or large-format applications than for the primary identity.

## Recommended next step

Choose one primary cue and, at most, one specialist alternate. Reconstruct only those approved custom glyphs as clean vector geometry against a single fixed typographic base. Do not trace the image-generated letters or ship the raster proofs as logos.
