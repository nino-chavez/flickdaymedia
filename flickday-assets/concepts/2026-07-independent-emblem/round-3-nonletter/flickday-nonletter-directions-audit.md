# Flickday non-letter directions — audit

## Reset

The stylized lowercase `f` territory is retired. At avatar size it sits too close to the visual territory associated with Facebook/Meta, regardless of the intended cut or motion behavior.

This round therefore excludes initials, cameras, shutters, lenses, play buttons, reels, and filmstrips.

## What the exercise revealed

### 01 — Scene shift

Useful as a transition or layout device, but the two vertical forms can resemble a pause button. Do not adopt as a standalone emblem without a more distinctive construction.

### 02 — Flashcut

The strongest standalone mark in this round. It has energy, works in one color, and can animate through four quick beats. Its weakness is category familiarity: four-part bursts and sparkles are common in current identity systems.

### 03 — Dayline

Connects naturally to “day,” light, and image-making. The static result reads too readily as sunrise, sunset, hospitality, or wellness. It may be useful as an editorial campaign graphic, not the core emblem.

### 04 — Moment signal

Has motion and focus, but can be interpreted as an eye, orbit, or wireless signal. Too ambiguous for the central mark.

### 05 — Cut stack

Strongest as a repeatable graphic system. The shared editorial seam can extend through photos, titles, transitions, lower thirds, apparel patterns, and website sections. As a standalone icon it risks reading as audio, analytics, or an equalizer.

### 06 — Corner hit

Useful social-layout vocabulary, but layered tiles and crop corners are already common. Better as a supporting template device than a logo.

## Recommendation

Stop treating “we need an emblem” as a fixed requirement.

Build Flickday around:

1. The clean wordmark as the only primary identifier.
2. A system of editorial shifts, seams, and staggered blocks derived from **Cut stack**.
3. A restrained standalone symbol only if a later concept demonstrates genuine distinctiveness at 32 px.
4. The existing play-`d` and aperture-`i` versions as service-specific campaign variants, not parts of the master logo.

This creates a more contemporary identity than forcing another camera-adjacent icon. It also produces more useful material for CapCut, social layouts, website transitions, and apparel.

## Next design round

Develop three system-first applications rather than more isolated icon thumbnails:

- motion: a 0.5–1.0 second cut-stack transition and watermark entrance;
- editorial: a social/post and website crop system using the shared seam;
- apparel: a large abstract back graphic paired with a small clean wordmark.

Only promote a shape to emblem status if it still feels proprietary across all three applications.
